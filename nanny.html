Baby Sai's Care Guide

Welcome to the household! We're grateful for your help — thank you for taking care of our baby boy. He's a sweet little guy who loves smiling.

This guide covers what you need for a shift with baby Sai. Read it through once before your first visit, then use the links below to jump to anything you need.

Parking

This townhome has two sides: the front of the house, and the back, where the garage is. Park in front of the house — street parking here is free.

Arrival

  • Wash your hands as soon as you get in — his immune system is still developing.
  • The house has three floors. Baby Sai's changing station and dresser are upstairs in the nursery, and the laundry machine is also upstairs. His bassinet is in the master bedroom, across the hallway from the nursery.
  • The guest bathroom on the bottom floor is yours to use.
  • Snacks and drinks on the counter and in the fridge are yours too — help yourself any time.

Wake windows

60–90 minutes awake before he's ready to wind down, at his current age

Track how long it's been since his last nap ended, and start the wind-down routine as he approaches that range. Catching the window matters more than the clock — an overtired baby fights sleep harder.

If a nap runs shorter than 30 minutes, treat the next wake window as shorter too — maybe under 60 minutes.

Feeding

If mom is home, she'll breastfeed baby Sai directly. If she's unavailable — on a call, out of the house — warm up milk or use formula; either is fine.

If he's hungry and crying hard, formula is usually the faster option, since the electric kettle heats water quickly. Start with 2 oz and offer more if he wants it.

Warming breastmilk from the fridge door takes about 4 minutes. Warm whichever bag is dated oldest first.

Clean bottles are stored next to the bottle washing machine, right by the sink. There's also an electric bottle warmer if you'd rather use it directly on a bottle.

Heads up — Baby Sai is almost exclusively breastfed and may reject the bottle entirely, formula or breastmilk. That's fine if it happens.

If mom will be back within two hours, it's fine to wait for her. If she'll be gone longer, try the other option — formula, then breastmilk, or vice versa — if he keeps refusing. He's usually hungry about every two hours, so keep offering periodically.

Diaper changes

The changing station is the dresser in the nursery, shown below.

Used diapers go in the Diaper Genie next to the dresser.

Heads up — After a poop diaper, apply Vaseline before closing the new one.

Sleep & swaddle

Baby Sai sleeps in a Love to Dream transition swaddle. For the first nap of the day, leave one arm out; for the second nap, leave the other arm out.

If he's fighting sleep, pick him up and bounce gently on the yoga ball. Turn the sound machine to its second setting — the combination usually settles him.

Baby Sai is a contact napper. If a nap runs short — 10 to 20 minutes — consider holding him through it instead of waiting him out. One full sleep cycle is close to 40–45 minutes.

Routine tasks

  • A laundry pod should already be pulled out; if not, extras are in the hallway storage. Run the load, then pull out anything that can't go in the dryer and hang it on the rack beside it. Once dry, sort into his dresser under the changing station — 0–3m on the top left, 3–6m on the top right, burp cloths middle left.

  • The machine is usually pre-loaded with dirty bottles. Add a tablet, fill the water tank to the max line — an underfilled tank can interrupt the cycle — and press Standard to start.
